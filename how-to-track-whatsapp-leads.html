How do you know where your WhatsApp leads come from?

October 14, 2026 · 5 min read · Di Pierro Creative

The direct answer

WhatsApp doesn't tell you where a contact came from. To find out, you measure in two places: at the click and in the conversation. At the click, every WhatsApp button on your site fires a GA4 event, and every link you share carries UTMs, so GA4 knows the channel. In the conversation, each channel uses a different pre-filled message, so you can tell the source as soon as it arrives. Add a simple log of who became a customer, and you'll know which channels bring customers, not just clicks.

Why WhatsApp is a blind spot

Most businesses that sell over WhatsApp measure well up to the click and poorly after it. GA4 sees the visit, the ad platform sees the click, but the conversation happens in an app that reports back to neither. The result is familiar: plenty of spend on channels that drive clicks, and little certainty about which ones drive sales.

The fix isn't a magic tool. It's building measurement as a system: every step leaves a mark the next step can read.

Step by step

  1. Standardize your UTMs

    Every link you share that points to your site gets three parameters: where it comes from, through which medium, and for which campaign. For example: ?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=bio&utm_campaign=october. The rule: always lowercase, and always the same name for the same channel. "Instagram", "insta" and "IG" become three different channels in your reports.

  2. Turn the click into a GA4 event

    Every WhatsApp button on your site should fire an event when clicked. Google recommends the name generate_lead for this. Mark that event as a key event in GA4: that's what makes it count as a conversion, including in Google Ads. If your site has several buttons, send along which service or page produced the click.

  3. Use one pre-filled message per channel

    A WhatsApp link can carry pre-filled text (wa.me/COUNTRYCODENUMBER?text=...). Use different text for each place the link appears. When the conversation arrives, the first line already tells you the source.

    Where the link isPre-filled message
    Website buttonHi! I found you through the website and wanted to ask about…
    Instagram bioHi! I came from Instagram.
    Google Business ProfileHi! I found you on Google.
    AdHi! I saw your October ad.

  4. Tag the source in the conversation

    In WhatsApp Business, create labels for each source (Website, Instagram, Google, Referral) and apply them as soon as a conversation comes in. When the sale closes, add a "Customer" label. At higher volume, a CRM connected to WhatsApp does this automatically.

  5. Log who became a customer, and review weekly

    A spreadsheet is enough to start: date, source, got a quote, became a sale, value. Every week, look at three numbers per channel: leads, close rate and revenue. That's where the decision that matters shows up, because the channel with the most clicks is rarely the one that sells the most.

The most common mistake: WhatsApp straight from the bio

If your Instagram bio links straight to WhatsApp, GA4 never sees that person: they never touched your site. Only the pre-filled message and the label say they came from Instagram. That's why step 3 isn't optional, and it's what separates "we think Instagram works" from "Instagram brought in 12 customers this month".

Can you do it yourself? Up to a point

Yes, and it's worth trying. Steps 1 to 3 fit into an afternoon if you have one channel and a simple site. Each piece, on its own, is easy.

The hard part is the system: the pieces have to keep talking to each other. The UTM has to match the name in your reports. The GA4 event has to survive the next change to your site. The WhatsApp label has to be applied by everyone who answers. And someone has to read the numbers every week and change what isn't working. That's where do-it-yourself tracking usually stalls: not on day one, but in month three.

Complexity grows fast once you add several channels, paid ads and a CRM. That's when the next step appears: sending back to Google Ads and Meta which conversations became sales (offline conversion import), so the algorithm looks for people like your buyers, not just your clickers.

Frequently asked questions

Can GA4 see the WhatsApp conversation?

No. GA4 sees up to the click on the button. What happens inside WhatsApp stays outside it, which is why a pre-filled message per channel and a sales log complete the picture.

Do I need the official WhatsApp API for this?

Not to start. Links with pre-filled messages, WhatsApp Business labels and a spreadsheet are enough. The API, through a CRM, becomes worth it when there are too many conversations to log by hand.

What if someone deletes the pre-filled message before sending?

It happens, but most people send it as is. For conversations with no source, ask in your first reply how they found you, and write it down.

Can I do this myself, or do I need a specialist?

You can do the basics yourself: UTMs, one GA4 event and a pre-filled message per channel. A specialist makes the difference once several channels, ads and a CRM are involved, and when someone needs to keep everything working and read the numbers to make decisions. Tracking is a system, and what usually fails is the connection between the pieces, not the pieces themselves.
